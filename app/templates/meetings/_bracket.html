{# The qualifying bracket. Read down the page in the order it happened rather than drawn as a left-to-right tree: a tree is four columns wide before a name is set, and a phone has room for about two. The structure needs no lines to be legible — a driver only appears in the next stage if they won this one, and the losing row of every pair is dimmed and terminal. **Each section is a band that opens.** Closed, the four summaries are an index of the session — two groups, four duels, two, one — which is the shape of qualifying legible in four lines at 360px before a single name is read. The last section is open by default, and on a completed round that is the Final, which is where "who took pole" is answered. On a Saturday afternoon with only the groups in, the last section *is* the groups, so the default lands on the newest thing either way without this file knowing which case it is in. **Three levels of ground, and no two of them the same.** The page is paper, the section is recessed, and each stage inside it inverts back to paper — which is the rule `tokens.css` already states for a control inside a panel, applied at a different scale. That inversion is what does the grouping: a duel is one white band with two rows in it, so four quarter-finals read as four objects and Group A cannot be mistaken for the top of Group B. Space alone was not doing it, because a row here is two lines tall and the gap between pairs never looked bigger than the gap inside one. **The rubric belongs to the section, not to a stage.** It sits on the band directly under the summary, so it names the columns once and every stage below it is the same shape. Putting it inside the first stage made that stage taller than its siblings, which is exactly the asymmetry the pairing change was trying to remove. **No duel headings and no elimination rule.** Both were removed in Phase 8.3 and the reasons are in `bracket.py`'s docstring. #} {% macro row(ui, bridge, palette, r, show_position, profile_base, profile_hx, yours, card_hx) %}
